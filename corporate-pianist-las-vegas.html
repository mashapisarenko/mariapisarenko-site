Where it works

Business events, and the rooms around them

Receptions & cocktail hours

The first hour sets the tone for everything after it. Live piano fills a room at a volume that lets people hear one another — which is the entire point of a reception.

Conventions & trade shows

On a floor built out of screens and noise, an acoustic instrument is the contrast. Played in a lounge, a suite or an exhibitor space, it slows people down and holds them longer than a monitor does.

Hospitality & VIP suites

Where the real conversations happen. A pianist in the corner of a suite signals care without a single word of branding.

Award evenings & galas

Arrival music, the settling of the room before the programme, and interludes between speakers. Timed to the run of show, not to a setlist.

Client & executive dinners

Small rooms, quiet playing. The music is there to make the table feel considered, and to disappear when it should.

Launches, openings & company parties

Product launches, grand openings, anniversaries and holiday parties — anywhere a company wants the evening to feel like an occasion rather than a booking.

Why a concert pianist

The difference your guests can hear

Most event music is chosen to be inoffensive. That is a low bar, and guests feel it. A performer trained on concert stages plays the same familiar repertoire with phrasing, dynamics and control — which is why a room goes quieter rather than louder when the playing is good.

It also means the programme can move. A reception that starts at Satie and Debussy can end at Rachmaninoff if the evening earns it. Nothing has to be pre-recorded, and nothing has to be repeated.

For corporate clients this matters in a specific way: the pianist is a credential in the room. A DMA, international competition prizes and concerto appearances with orchestra are things your guests can be told, and then hear confirmed.

Questions

What organisers ask before they book

What kinds of corporate events do you play?

Receptions and opening evenings, conventions and trade shows, hospitality suites, award evenings, client dinners, product launches and galas — anywhere the room is meant to feel considered rather than merely filled with sound.

Does the venue need to have a piano?

Yes — an acoustic piano at the venue is required. I play the instrument that is in the room; I do not bring one of my own, and I do not perform on an electronic keyboard. Ask your venue what they have, and if there is no piano, one can be hired in for the day. Either way, tell me early, so the programme is prepared for that particular instrument.

Is there a minimum booking?

Two hours is the minimum booking. Travel and any breaks within the engagement are part of the booked time and are included in the quote.

Can you play for a whole convention day, or only an evening?

That is arranged with you personally rather than sold as a fixed package. Tell me the shape of your day — when music should begin, when it should stop, and what happens in between — and I will propose the playing and the breaks around it. Breaks fall inside the booked time, so the programme runs to your schedule, not to mine.

Can you include a piece connected to our company or our guest of honour?

Yes. One requested piece is prepared in advance as part of every booking. If you would like more than one, ask early and we will plan it into the programme.

How far in advance should we book?

As early as you can. The date is held for one event only, so the earlier you write, the more likely your date is free and the more time there is to prepare a programme, and any requested piece, properly for your room.

Do you take a deposit?

Yes. A retainer reserves your date and takes it off the calendar for anyone else; the balance and the cancellation terms are confirmed in writing together with your quote.

Do you play outside the Las Vegas valley?

I play throughout the Las Vegas valley — Las Vegas, Henderson, Summerlin, Enterprise and Spring Valley — and I travel beyond it for the right event. Travel is included in the quote.

Do you play with other musicians?

Solo classical piano is the booking — that is what I do and what the programme is built around. A duo with violin or cello, accompaniment for a singer, or a small ensemble can be added by request, booked well in advance and priced accordingly, but the piano is the centre of the evening.

What do you wear?

Concert attire by default. Black tie, all black, or something quieter on request — tell me what the room calls for.

How much does it cost?

Every event is quoted individually, so there is no price list. To prepare a quote I need the date, the start time and how long you would like music; the venue, and whether a piano is already there; the occasion and the mood you have in mind; and any piece that must be included. This is a concert engagement, not background cover — the programme is prepared for your room, the playing is at concert level, and the quote reflects that.
