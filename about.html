Biography

From a Siberian prodigy to the world’s stages

Recognised as a child prodigy in Siberia, Maria Pisarenko began the piano at five, gave her first solo recital at seven, and appeared as a concerto soloist with symphony orchestras by eight. After top honours at the First International Tchaikovsky Youth Competition in Moscow, she was invited to the Central Music School of the Moscow Tchaikovsky Conservatory — the heart of the Russian school of piano playing.

She went on to degrees in Piano Performance, Pedagogy and Collaborative Piano at the Gnesin Russian Academy of Music, and to a Doctor of Musical Arts at the University of Nevada, Las Vegas — alongside a Ph.D. in Linguistics.

At the Gnesins Academy she studied with Dr. Lina Bulatova, Dr. Vera Nossina and Professor Yuri Ponizovkin — a pupil of the legendary Maria Yudina and a noted Rachmaninoff scholar. She has worked in lessons and masterclasses with legends of the school — Eliso Virsaladze, Vera Gornostayeva, Sergei Dorensky, Lev Naumov and Vladimir Krainev — with consultations at The Juilliard School.

The lineage

A teaching line back to Franz Liszt

In the Russian school a pianist is shaped by a teacher, who was shaped by a teacher. Maria’s line is direct and documented — and it passes through some of the most important musicians of the last two centuries.

  1. Franz Liszt 1811–1886

    The greatest pianist of the nineteenth century, and the teacher from whom the modern piano tradition descends. From 1848 he lived and taught in Weimar, where pianists from all over Europe came to study with him. Read more

  2. Alexander Siloti 1863–1945

    Studied with Liszt in Weimar. Returning to Russia in 1887, he taught at the Moscow Conservatory, where his students included Alexander Goldenweiser — and his own cousin, Sergei Rachmaninoff. Read more

  3. Alexander Goldenweiser 1875–1961

    Pianist, composer and one of the great professors of the Moscow Conservatory. He studied with Siloti and Pavel Pabst; his pupils included Samuil Feinberg, Grigory Ginzburg, Lazar Berman, Dmitri Bashkirov and Tatiana Nikolayeva. Read more

  4. Tatiana Nikolayeva 1924–1993 · Ilya Klyachko

    Two of Goldenweiser’s pupils. Nikolayeva won first prize at the International Bach Competition in Leipzig in 1950, and Shostakovich composed and dedicated his 24 Preludes and Fugues, Op. 87, to her. Ilya Klyachko, also Goldenweiser’s pupil, was one of Maria’s regular teachers at the Central Music School. Read about Nikolayeva

  5. Manana Kandelaki

    A pupil of Tatiana Nikolayeva, and Maria’s formative teacher at the Central Music School of the Moscow Conservatory.

  6. Dr. Maria Pisarenko

    So the line runs twice to Goldenweiser — through Nikolayeva and Kandelaki, and through Klyachko — and through Siloti to Liszt himself. As a student she also played for Nikolayeva in person.

The wider school she belongs to is the one of Heinrich Neuhaus (1888–1964), teacher of Sviatoslav Richter and Emil Gilels and author of The Art of Piano Playing, and of Maria Yudina (1899–1970) — through Professor Ponizovkin, Yudina’s pupil, and through masterclasses with Neuhaus’s own students Lev Naumov, Vera Gornostayeva and Vladimir Krainev.

Where she trained

The schools behind the playing

Central Music School of the Moscow Conservatory

Founded in 1935 as the Moscow Conservatory’s own school for exceptionally gifted children, and the starting point of a long list of the world’s leading pianists. Maria was invited there after top honours at the First International Tchaikovsky Youth Competition in Moscow. Official site (in Russian)

Moscow P. I. Tchaikovsky State Conservatory

Founded in 1866 by Nikolai Rubinstein; Tchaikovsky himself taught there. The conservatory of Siloti, Goldenweiser, Neuhaus and Nikolayeva — the institution the Central Music School belongs to. Official site

Gnesin Russian Academy of Music

One of Russia’s leading music academies, founded in 1895. Maria earned degrees there in Piano Performance, Pedagogy and Collaborative Piano. Official site

University of Nevada, Las Vegas

Doctor of Musical Arts in piano — alongside a Ph.D. in Linguistics. Concerto soloist with the UNLV Symphony Orchestra under Taras Krysa.
